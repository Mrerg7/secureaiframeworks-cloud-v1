"Why does that domain cost so much?" is the wrong question. The right question is what a name is worth **to the buyer who needs it** — and that number is more predictable than most people assume.

Professional brokers weigh the same seven factors over and over.

## Factor 1: How well it matches buyer intent

A domain that describes exactly what a company sells does part of the marketing before a visitor ever reads a headline. `secureaiframeworks.cloud` tells a CISO everything in one glance: this is about secure AI frameworks.

Descriptive, category-level names outperform clever puns because they capture **high-intent traffic** — people already searching for that solution.

## Factor 2: Length and spelling

Every additional character is friction. The thresholds that matter in practice:

- **1–2 words, easy to spell:** premium tier.
- **Hyphens, numbers, or awkward spellings:** meaningful discount.
- **Unpronounceable or easily confused strings:** strong discount.

If you have to spell your own domain over the phone, the name is working against you.

## Factor 3: The extension

`.com` still carries the most weight for global consumer brands. But for infrastructure, developer, and platform companies, well-chosen alternatives — `.cloud`, `.io`, `.ai`, `.dev` — are native and expected.

What matters is whether the extension reinforces the business. Cloud infrastructure on `.cloud` reads as deliberate; a mismatch reads as a fallback.

## Factor 4: Commercial intent of the keywords

Words tied to money — *security*, *compliance*, *enterprise*, *platform*, *pay* — attract buyers with budgets. A domain in a high-value vertical supports a higher price because the return on acquisition is higher.

## Factor 5: Comparable sales

Brokerage reports and public sales data give a floor and a ceiling. Recent comparable transactions in the same keyword family are the single strongest pricing signal.

## Factor 6: Development readiness

A name that can host a real product, marketing site, and `hello@` address today is worth more than one that needs rebranding around it. Email branding alone — professional addresses on your own domain — is a recurring, compounding benefit.

## Factor 7: Scarcity of alternatives

There is exactly one `secureaiframeworks.cloud`. When a category has a natural exact-match name and only one owner can hold it, the price reflects that monopoly on the phrase.

## Putting it together

No single factor sets the price; the combination does. Strong intent + short length + correct extension + expensive vertical + clean comparables is what separates a five-figure name from a fifteen-dollar registration.
